# Research and creative thesis — v3

## What the research found
1. **The practice's real subject is Vidarbha's working economy, not "architecture in Nagpur".** Its client list (12 public bodies) reads like a supply chain:
   - agricultural produce market committees (5 APMCs)
   - a cotton ginning and pressing co-operative (Saoner) and a cotton spinning mill co-operative (Soot Girni, Nagpur)
   - education societies, state police housing, community and civic halls
   - developers' flat schemes and townships
   - bungalows and interiors

   Katol, one of the APMC towns, is described as a town where "cotton is the staple" of trade (Wikipedia, Katol).
2. **Geography is proof.** Every public client is a named town at a measurable distance from Nagpur. Nagpur's Zero Mile Stone, the colonial survey marker for the centre of India, gives a ready-made, local and unarguable origin point.
3. **The practice works in Marathi as well as English.** Its own renderings carry Devanagari signage (उषःकाल-9, सभागृह तथा प्रशिक्षण केंद्र). Its public clients are Marathi-medium institutions.
4. **The assets:**
   - about 50 renderings and 15 interior photographs, all low-resolution (500–1000 px)
   - two drawing title blocks with verifiable project facts
   - the logo (charcoal and coral), and portraits of both architects
5. **The competitor look.** Nagpur studios listed in directories (Re-thinking The Future "Top 25 Architecture Firms in Nagpur") present as generic "architect in Nagpur" brochure sites: slider hero, service icons, gallery. Larger Indian practices (Morphogenesis, Sanjay Puri Architects) lead with full-bleed hero imagery and sliders.

## Creative thesis
**What it should feel like:** a surveyor's notebook kept by a practice that has driven out to every market yard in the district. Measured, bilingual, specific, slightly old-fashioned in its care.

**What it must say:** for 46 years this practice has designed the buildings that Vidarbha's farm economy and its growing city run on, from the market yard where the harvest arrives to the family home at the end of the chain.
- **Proof 1:** five APMCs and two cotton co-operatives on the client list.
- **Proof 2:** real drawings with title blocks.
- **Proof 3:** two generations of architects.

**How the visual language carries it:**
- **The Zero Mile map:** an origin point, real distances, approximate and labelled as such.
- **The बाजार → गिरणी → शाळा → शहर → घर chain:** organises the entire homepage and the enquiry form.
- **Type:** Tiro Devanagari Marathi is a single family designed for both Marathi and English, so the two scripts are set as equals. Mukta is used for body text, and Plex Mono for distances and codes.
- **Colour:** cotton-white paper, black-cotton-soil ink and the logo's coral.

**This, not that:**
- Distances, not "serving Nagpur and beyond".
- The client's chain, not a list of services.
- Marathi words that mean something, not ornament.
- Labelled renderings, not implied photographs.
- An index, not a slider.

## Hero revision (research)
- **What the first version lacked.** It had no real work above the fold, and an abstract map asked visitors to interpret it before they knew what the practice does. Architecture-homepage guidance favours one strong visual statement of the work, the positioning in plain words, and one clear next step (nicepage, "Architecture & Building Homepage Designs").
- **The milestone motif.** Indian roadside milestones are white stones with a coloured cap: yellow for a national highway, green for a state highway, blue or black for city and district roads, and orange for a village road (rural roads / PMGSY). Sources: OrissaPOST, Udayavani, Civil4M. An orange-capped stone is the roadside marker for the village roads that lead to the APMC towns. The logo's coral is close to that orange, so the brand colour and the local reference coincide.
- **The interaction.** The hovered or focused stone widens to show its work; nothing auto-plays. It works by keyboard, and the motion is disabled under reduced-motion settings.

## Hero, third revision: Dribbble and Behance research (27 Nov 2026)
I reviewed Dribbble's "architecture website hero" results and Behance's "architecture studio website" projects.

**Recurring patterns that work:**
- **Giant wordmark with the building in front.** An oversized word sits behind the building and the building overlaps it ("SOLA", "NORDEN", "BEYOND", "EMBER").
- **One real building as the hero.** A single building, not a collage.
- **Little text.** A short headline bottom-left.
- **A floating project card.** A card with a thumbnail and "View project" sits bottom-right.
- **Technical annotation.** ZÁMOK uses coordinates and degree marks with an orange accent.

**What I took, made local:**
- **The word is नागपूर, in the site's Devanagari face and the logo's coral.**
- **The building in front is the practice's own.** It is Manjusha Regalia (the name is on the rendering's signage); I removed the sky from their rendering with a colour-based mask and feathered the ground and edges.
- **The annotation is Nagpur's coordinates, 21.15° N · 79.08° E**, a quiet echo of Zero Mile.
- **The floating card leads to the APMC case study.**

**What I did not take:**
- Full-bleed stock-style photography (the practice's images are low-resolution renderings).
- Glassmorphism and dark luxury palettes.
